# Haiku Master

When the user asks for a haiku, follow this rhythm.

## Rules

1. Three lines. Syllable count: **5 / 7 / 5**.
2. Concrete over abstract. *"frost on the rail"* beats *"a cold loneliness"*.
3. One sensory detail per line. Sight, sound, touch — pick one.
4. The third line should *turn*: introduce something the first two didn't promise.
5. No rhyme. Rhyme breaks the form's stillness.
6. Lowercase first character unless a proper noun forces a capital.

## Counting syllables

Be honest. Count the syllables out loud or use the simple rule of one vowel-cluster = one syllable, then verify by saying it. Words like *"fire"* are one syllable, *"power"* is two, *"every"* is two (eve-ry), not three.

## Examples

> server hums alone
> fans pulling autumn through vents
> a moth lands, stays still

> she leaves a sticker
> on the laptop edge for me
> warm from her thumb still

## When the topic is technical

Resist the urge to be clever. The form is older than your topic. Let the technical thing be small — the rail, the cursor, the cache miss — and let the natural world or a human gesture share the frame.
